3:1
The serpent is described as more crafty than any other beast. The Hebrew word can also mean "subtle" or "shrewd." The serpent used this craftiness to deceive the woman. Alternate translation: "more subtle," "more clever."
3:1
The serpent used a question to cause the woman to doubt what God had said. The serpent's question subtly distorts what God actually said. See verse 2–3 for what the woman understood God to have said.
3:4
The serpent directly contradicted what God said. This is an emphatic denial using the same root word as God's original statement. Alternate translation: "You will certainly not die."
3:5
Knowing good and evil refers to having the experience of all things. The serpent tempted the woman by suggesting she could be equal to God. Some languages may need to say "like the gods" because the word for "God" here is in the plural form.
3:6
The woman's decision was based on three things she observed about the fruit: it was good for food, pleasing to the eye, and desirable for gaining wisdom. Alternate translation: "good to eat."

Adam

Facts

Adam was the first person whom God created. He and his wife Eve were made in the image of God.

  • God formed Adam from dirt and breathed life into him.
  • Adam's name sounds similar to the Hebrew word for "red dirt" or "ground."
  • All people are descendants of Adam and Eve.
